    Lessons

    Lesson · 1RMDo you need to know your max?

    Your 1RM, one rep max, is the most you can lift once with good form. It's a useful number, and that's all it is.

    You don't train at it. On Strength For Life you train at your 7RM, the most you can lift for 7 good reps, and that's the number that moves you forward.

    So why max out to find something a sum can estimate? Hit your 7, put it in here and get on with your day.

    Lesson · AccuracyWhy this stops at 10 reps

    The more reps you put in, the rougher the guess. In one study, a 5RM predicted a real 1RM best, and accuracy fell away at 10 and 20 reps. The authors advised using no more than 10 [1].

    Typical error, chest press
    From a 5RM: 1.8 kg
    From a 10RM: 3.6 kg

    Typical error, leg press
    From a 5RM: 13.5 kg
    From a 10RM: 23.8 kg

    That's from a 20-person check group, and those researchers built their own equations for each lift. They didn't test this formula. Their 70-person main group included untrained and trained men and women, aged 18 to 69, tested over two sessions [1].

    Your 7 sits between 5 and 10... Close enough to be useful, and still a guess.

    Lesson · PercentagesWhy 7 reps doesn't mean the same on every lift

    This formula says your 7RM is 81% of your 1RM. Real people don't follow one chart.

    A review of 269 studies looked at how many reps people get at each percentage. At 80% of 1RM, the average was 8.8 reps on bench press and 13.1 on leg press [2].

    So a leg press estimate here can read high. Compare the same lift with itself over time, and don't compare one lift with another.

    Most of that data came from healthy adults under 59, and 60% of the groups were resistance trained. Sex, age and training status made no clear difference. These were one-off rep tests, not training studies [2].

    Lesson · Why 7There's no magic rep range

    A 2021 review argued that you can build muscle and strength across many loading zones, rather than one perfect range [3]. It's a review of existing research, not a single trial.

    So 7 isn't magic. It's a line in the sand. Heavy enough to build strength, light enough to lift with good form and simple enough that you always know the job.

    One top set. Hit the Magnificent 7. The weight goes up... Never by more than 5%.

    Lesson · FailureGood reps only

    Only count reps you'd be happy for me to watch. A swung, bounced or half rep will inflate your number and tell you nothing.

    You don't need to grind every set either. A review of 15 studies found no evidence that going to momentary failure builds more muscle than stopping short of it [4]. Counting any definition of failure, the advantage was trivial [4].

    That review covered muscle growth, not strength. It included healthy adults of any training experience.

    The objective of training is adaptation, not fatigue.
