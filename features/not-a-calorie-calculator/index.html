Lesson · BMR & RMRWhat does your body burn doing nothing?

BMR, basal metabolic rate, is what your body burns just to stay alive. Heart, lungs, brain, organs. Measured lying still, fasted, in a warm room after a night's sleep.

RMR, resting metabolic rate, is the same idea measured under looser conditions, so it reads slightly higher. It's what labs actually measure and what the equations predict.

It's the biggest slice of your day, typically 60 to 70% of everything you burn [18]. The catch... The equations predict an average, not you. That's why we blend three of them and give you a range.

Lesson · TEF & metabolic costWhy protein is the cheapest calorie you can eat

TEF, the thermic effect of food, is the energy it costs to digest, absorb and store what you eat. Around 10% of a normal day's burn [18].

But it isn't flat. Protein costs 20 to 30% of its own calories to process. Carbs 5 to 10%. Fat 0 to 3% [11]. That's the metabolic cost of food.

100 kcal of protein ≈ 75 kcal kept
100 kcal of carbs ≈ 92 kcal kept
100 kcal of fat ≈ 98 kcal kept

Same number on the label. Very different number in you... Protein also fills you up more than anything else on the plate [11]. And real food costs more to process than factory food [12].

Lesson · NEATThe burn nobody counts

NEAT, non-exercise activity thermogenesis, is everything you burn moving that isn't planned training. Walking the dog. Chasing the kids. Stairs, standing, fidgeting.

It's the most variable part of anyone's day. Two people of a similar size can differ by up to 2,000 calories a day [17]... That's why we score your life in real hours instead of a tick box.

Lesson · Protein, lifting & MPSWhy lifting and protein beat cardio for losing fat, not muscle

Cardio burns more in the session. No argument. In an eight-month trial in overweight, previously sedentary adults, cardio lost more weight and fat than lifting alone. But only the lifters gained muscle, and the best result came from doing both [23].

Here's what the scale doesn't tell you. On a very low calorie diet, the group doing cardio saw their resting burn drop. The group lifting on the same diet kept their lean mass and their resting burn [22].

Add protein and it gets better. Young men on a 40% calorie deficit, all lifting and doing intervals, ate either 1.2 or 2.4g of protein per kg. In four weeks the high protein group lost 4.8kg of fat and gained 1.2kg of lean mass. The lower protein group lost 3.5kg of fat and gained next to none [21].

MPS, muscle protein synthesis, is how your body repairs and builds muscle. Lifting switches it on, and it stays raised for up to 48 hours after a session [24]. Protein is the raw material. And protein is the most expensive food to process, costing 20 to 30% of its own calories in TEF [11].

Lift → MPS switched on for up to 48 hours
Protein → the building blocks, plus the highest TEF
Together → the weight you lose is fat, not muscle

Muscle is the engine. Each kilo burns about 13 calories a day at rest, fat about 4.5 [25]. Small on a daily basis... But muscle is what keeps you strong, moving and looking how you want once the weight comes off.

Lose weight with cardio alone and some of it can be muscle. Lose it lifting and eating protein and it's fat. Cardio is a bonus, not the plan.

Lesson · EATTrain for what it builds, not what it burns

EAT, exercise activity thermogenesis, is what you burn in planned training. For most people it's a smaller slice than they think. Even in people who train hard, it tops out around 15 to 30% of the day's burn [17].

Your watch overstates it. Wrist trackers have been out by 27 to 93% on calories burned [19]. And your body claws back around 28% by turning its resting burn down [10].

Lift to build muscle and strength. That's what changes your engine for good.

Lesson · EPOCThe afterburn, and the bollox sold about it

EPOC, excess post-exercise oxygen consumption, is the afterburn. After training your body keeps using extra oxygen to refuel, repair and cool down.

It's real, but small... Only 6 to 15% of the energy cost of the session itself [16]. Harder sessions sit at the top of that range.

Anyone selling you a 24-hour fat-burning afterburn from cardio is selling you fluff and bollox. We add it in, scaled to how hard you train.

Lifting is different. Its afterburn is bigger than steady cardio of a similar energy cost [28]. And then the repair starts. Resting burn stays raised by about 5% for up to 72 hours after a single lifting session [26], and has been measured 4.7 to 9.4% higher the next morning [27].

A big part of that is MPS, rebuilding muscle protein. Making protein is expensive... Protein turnover uses around a fifth of your resting energy [30]. That repair cost sits outside EPOC, so we count it separately as Repair & rebuild.

Lesson · The (NOT a)calorie deficitWhy so many people undereat, and fall off the wagon

The (NOT a)calorie deficit is the deficit you're actually running, not the one you were told you're running. Your target came from an obsolete equation, built over 100 years ago on an already invalid metric, multiplied by a tick box [1][20].

That sum leaves out the metabolic cost of what you actually do. The digestion cost of protein [11]. The repair and rebuild after lifting [26]. The extra muscle you carry [25]. Your job and your life [17]. And even the best equation misses your resting burn by more than 10% for plenty of people [4].

So the people doing everything right, lifting and eating high protein, are often the ones the sum shortchanges most. The PT plans a sensible 20% cut. The real one is bigger.

A bigger deficit isn't a faster win. Lose faster than about 0.5 to 1% of bodyweight a week and you risk losing muscle with the fat [31]. And appetite fights back hard... For every kilo lost, the drive to eat rose by around 100 calories a day in one trial, more than three times the drop in calories burned [32].

Hungry, tired, losing strength, scale stalling. That's when people fall off the wagon and blame themselves. In 35 years of coaching, the problem is rarely willpower... It's a deficit nobody did the maths on properly.

Lesson · Recovery & collagenUnder-fuelled, under-recovered: why it catches up with you

Training doesn't just break down muscle. Tendons, ligaments and other connective tissue take the load too, and they're built from collagen.

Rebuilding collagen needs raw materials. Protein supplies the building blocks. Vitamin C is a cofactor for the enzymes that stabilise the collagen molecule [34]. In a small study of 8 men, vitamin C-enriched gelatin taken an hour before exercise roughly doubled a blood marker of collagen synthesis [35].

Under-eat and muscle repair slows too. Five days at an energy deficit cut resting muscle protein synthesis by 27% in 15 young adults. Lifting plus protein brought it back [33].

Keep it up long enough and the bill grows. Low energy availability is linked to a poorer training response, lower strength and a higher injury risk, in men and women [36].

Every session you rebuild a little less than you broke down. Week on week it stacks up... Niggles, aches, stalled lifts, flat energy. That's cumulative systemic fatigue and under-recovery, and it's the hidden bill of a (NOT a)calorie deficit.

At least 1.6g protein per kg, every day
Collagen-rich food: slow-cooked cuts, bone broth, skin-on fish
Vitamin C: peppers, kiwi, citrus, berries, broccoli

Lesson · TDEEHow your day adds up

TDEE, total daily energy expenditure, is everything you burn in a day, added up.

TDEE = RMR + NEAT + EAT + EPOC + TEF

The bar above is yours, piece by piece. Look at which slice is biggest... It isn't the gym. That's why the gym is for building the engine, and the kitchen and your day decide the rest.

    The three engines

    Find your real number

    Eat the maintenance figure for three weeks. Weigh every morning and compare weekly averages, not single days. Weight flat? That's your number. Rising or falling? Move it by 100 to 200 a day and go again. The scale tells you what no equation can.

    The science behind every number

    Every figure in this calculator, where it comes from, and which calls are ours. Tap a reference to read the study.

    EvidenceTaken straight from published researchDW callOur working figure, built on the research
    Mifflin-St JeorEvidence
    10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age + 5 (men) or − 161 (women). The most reliable common equation for non-obese and obese adults, landing within 10% of measured resting burn more often than any other [3][4].
    Harris-BenedictEvidence
    Built on 239 healthy people in 1919 [1]. We use the 1984 revision, reckoned to land within about 14% in normally nourished people [2]. In athletes it showed no significant error, where Mifflin-St Jeor did [5].
    CunninghamEvidence
    500 + 22 × lean mass (kg). Built on lean mass, not total weight [6]. Also accurate in athletes [5]. Only used when we can estimate body fat.
    Body fat from a tapeEvidence
    Relative Fat Mass: 64 − 20 × height ÷ waist (+12 for women). Validated against DXA scans and more accurate than BMI [7].
    Blending the threeDW call
    Train 2+ lifting or 3+ cardio sessions a week and Harris-Benedict and Cunningham get 40% each, Mifflin-St Jeor 20%. Otherwise Mifflin-St Jeor leads at 50%. Weights follow the accuracy findings above [4][5], the split itself is ours.
    Activity costsEvidence
    Every activity uses its MET value from the 2024 Adult Compendium [8]. Lifestyle levels 1 to 5: 2.5 (pottering, light jobs), 3.0 (walking the dog), 4.0 (walking to work), 5.0 (brisk walking, chasing the kids, around the 4.8 for brisk walking and 5.8 for vigorous play), 6.5 (around the 6.0 for cross-country hiking and 6.8 for cycling to work). Desk work 1.5. Standing work 1.8. Trades 3.0. Labouring 4.0 to 5.0. Weights 3.5 to 5.8. Brisk walk 4.8. Rowing 7.3.
    Scaled to youDW call
    A MET assumes a standard resting burn, and real resting burn varies, which is why a separate Compendium exists for over-60s [8][9]. So one MET here equals your blended resting burn per kg per hour, and only energy above resting is added so nothing is counted twice.
    Shift averagesDW call
    Nobody digs for eight solid hours. Job values are shift averages from the Compendium's occupation codes [8] and then reduced 20% for breaks. Hard cardio intervals are averaged at 9.0 across the session, between moderate and vigorous interval values [8].
    The rest of the dayDW call
    8 hours asleep at resting burn. Any awake hours you didn't account for are scored at 1.5, the Compendium's level for light sitting tasks [8].
    Exercise clawbackEvidence
    Training burn is cut by 28%. Across 1,754 adults, the body offset an average 28% of extra activity energy by lowering resting burn [10].
    Digestion and proteinEvidence
    Digestion costs 20 to 30% of protein calories, 5 to 10% of carbs and 0 to 3% of fat [11]. We use the midpoints (25%, 7.5%, 2%), take your protein intake and split the rest evenly between carbs and fat.
    Ultra-processed foodDW call
    A processed meal cost almost half the digestive energy of a matched whole food one [12]. That is one small study, so we apply a modest version: digestion runs 15% higher on a scratch-cooked diet, 30% lower on an all-packet one.
    Hidden overeatingEvidence
    On an ultra-processed diet, people ate 508 kcal a day more than on whole food [13]. UK adults get 56.8% of their calories from ultra-processed food [14]. Flagged as a warning, not added to the maths.
    Protein's metabolic costEvidence
    Protein shown at 25% digestion cost, the midpoint of 20 to 30% [11]. So 100 kcal of protein leaves about 75, carbs about 92, fat about 98. Enter your grams for the most accurate figure.
    Lifting, protein & MPSEvidence
    Higher protein (2.4 vs 1.2g/kg) with hard training in a 40% deficit gave more fat loss and more lean mass gain in young men over 4 weeks [21]. Lifting on a very low calorie diet preserved lean mass and resting burn where cardio didn't [22]. Cardio alone lost more weight and fat than lifting alone over 8 months, but only lifting added muscle and combining both did best [23]. MPS stayed raised 48 hours after lifting in untrained people [24]. Muscle ≈13 and fat ≈4.5 kcal/kg/day at rest [25]. Shown as guidance, not added to the maths.
    The (NOT a)calorie deficitDW call
    "Standard calculator" here means Mifflin-St Jeor [3] times the usual activity factor picked from your weekly sessions: 1.2 for none, 1.375 for 1 to 3, 1.55 for 4 or 5, 1.725 for 6 or 7, 1.9 for 8 plus. We compare a 20% cut from that number with your NOT A Calorie Calculator burn. Weekly loss uses the common rule of thumb of about 7,700 kcal per kg, itself an estimate. Safe loss rate for keeping muscle of 0.5 to 1% of bodyweight a week [31]. Appetite rises around 100 kcal a day per kg lost [32]. The link to people quitting is Danny's coaching experience, not a measured result.
    Recovery, collagen & vitamin CEvidence
    Vitamin C is a cofactor for the collagen hydroxylases [34]. Vitamin C-enriched gelatin before exercise roughly doubled the collagen synthesis marker PINP in 8 men over 3 days [35]. A 5-day energy deficit cut resting MPS 27% in 15 young adults, restored by lifting and protein [33]. Low energy availability reduces training response and strength and raises injury risk [36]. "Cumulative systemic fatigue" is Danny's coaching description of how these add up over weeks, not a measured result. Shown as guidance, not added to the maths.
    Repair & rebuild after liftingDW call
    Resting burn rose about 5% (around 95 kcal a day) for 72 hours after one lifting session in 8 overweight young men [26], and 4.7 to 9.4% the next morning in trained men [27]. Lifting's afterburn beat steady cycling of similar cost [28]. Over months, lifting raised resting burn by about 96 kcal a day against controls, aerobic training showed no significant change [29], though the authors rate those studies as high risk of bias. We add 5% of resting burn for the 48 hours after each session (capped at every day), then take the 28% clawback off. Conservative, and small study numbers, so treat it as a guide.
    Lift or cardio comparisonDW call
    Compares 60 minutes of each using your own body and resting burn: session cost [8], afterburn [16][28] and, for lifting, 48 hours of repair at 5% [26]. Clawback left off both so they compare fairly. The verdict is Danny's coaching call, built on that and on what the weight you lose is made of [21][22][23].
    EPOC, the afterburnDW call
    EPOC runs at 6 to 15% of the net energy cost of a session, rising with intensity [16]. We use 6% for easy cardio, 10% for steady cardio and lifting, 15% for hard intervals, then apply the 28% clawback.
    How your day splitsEvidence
    Resting burn typically 60 to 70% of the day, digestion around 10% [18]. NEAT varies by up to 2,000 kcal a day between people of similar size, and exercise maxes out around 15 to 30% even in people who train [17].
    Protein targetEvidence
    At least 1.6g per kg of bodyweight a day, where gains in lean mass levelled off in the largest meta-analysis on protein and resistance training [15].
    The ±10% rangeEvidence
    Even the best equation misses by more than 10% in a good share of people [4], so you get a range, not one magic number.
    Goal targetsDW call
    20% and 10% under for fat loss, 10% over for building. Fat-loss targets never go below your resting burn. Coaching rules of thumb, then tested against the scale.
